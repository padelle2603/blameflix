# Manifesto — BlameFlix

> **Language / Lingua**: **English** (this document) | [Italiano](MANIFEST.md)

This document explains why BlameFlix exists, what its mission is and where it is
heading. It does not describe features or regulations: for those consult
[README.md](../README.md), [LEGAL_EN.md](LEGAL_EN.md) and
[PRIVACY_EN.md](PRIVACY_EN.md).

## 1. Why BlameFlix exists

Watching today is fragmented. A title on one platform, another on a second one,
a third on TV: every legal service is a closed universe, with its own catalog,
its own "watched / to watch" state and its own notifications. The platforms do
not talk to each other, and the memory of what we watch stays locked inside each
of them.

The result is that the user loses the thread of their viewing: they do not
remember where they watched a series, which season they reached, which episode
is waiting for them, when the next one airs. The platforms know every single
fragment, but none of them knows the whole picture.

BlameFlix was born for this: not to replace the platforms, but to live *on top*
of them, as a personal layer that reunites what is scattered. A tool that gives
the user back a single picture of their viewing, whatever the legal service
that hosts it.

## 2. Mission

BlameFlix's mission is to offer a **personal, shared catalog independent of the
platforms**: a single place to save, track and find again what you watch,
wherever you watch it.

BlameFlix is also a **viewing automation tool**: it remembers episodes and
seasons, keeps count of what is left to watch, follows release dates, generates
notifications when a new title or a new episode arrives and, if you configure a
source, takes you directly to the legal service you chose with a single tap.

The idea is simple: **the user chooses, BlameFlix remembers.** The management —
what has already been watched, what is released, what is waiting — is no longer
manual work spread across dozens of apps, but a single silent responsibility
entrusted to the catalog.

## 3. Vision

BlameFlix's vision is to become **the single source of truth for one's own
viewing**.

Not the content — that stays, and must stay, where it legally resides — but the
*knowledge* of the content: what exists, when it airs, what I have already
watched, where I left a series. BlameFlix does not own or carry the works; it
owns the map of what the user watches.

In this vision, the shared catalog is the intelligence layer on top of the legal
services: agnostic to the platform, private, entirely on the device. The
platforms remain what they are — places to watch — and BlameFlix becomes what
they are missing — a place to remember. The user moves freely between the legal
services they prefer without losing anything: their library, their progress,
their releases follow them.

In the long run, BlameFlix wants to be the home of personal viewing: the
discreet layer underneath every subscription and every streaming app, that asks
for no data, tracks no behavior and binds to no service. One single view of
yours, anywhere.

## 4. Who develops it

BlameFlix is a **personal** project, developed and maintained by a single
independent person. It is not a company, has no funding, is not distributed
commercially and earns nothing in any way.

There are no affiliations with streaming platforms, production companies,
distributors or other industry companies: BlameFlix is independent and stays
that way. Its independence is also a guarantee of neutrality — there is no
service to promote, no content to push, no company to please.

Being a private project, BlameFlix grows at the pace and by the criteria of its
developer, for the sole purpose of being useful: a personal tool made available
to those who share the same needs.

## 5. Guiding principles

- **Privacy by design**: no server, no backend, no account. The data stays on
  your device and in the backup files you create.
- **Neutrality**: BlameFlix does not provide, point to or know any content
  source. It is a tool, not a guide.
- **Legality**: the catalog is designed to work with authorized streaming
  services, subscriptions and legal platforms. The choice of where to watch is
  yours, and you are responsible for it.
- **Data locality**: everything you save lives on your device. Nothing is sent,
  collected or shared, apart from the normal requests to the services you use.
- **User freedom**: no service is imposed, no content is suggested, no platform
  is favored. BlameFlix adapts to you, not you to BlameFlix.

## 6. Conclusion

BlameFlix is a shared catalog on top of the legal platforms, a viewing
automation and a personal point of truth. It was born from a private need, grew
as an independent project, and lives according to the principles that define it:
privacy, neutrality, legality and user freedom.
